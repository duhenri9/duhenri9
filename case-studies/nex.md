# NEX — Governed AI operations

**Project:** WM3 Digital / NEX  
**Focus:** business-adaptive AI orchestration, authorised tool use, evaluations, human control and outcome evidence  
**Maturity:** Controlled architecture and runtime lab; workflow-first interface remains a deterministic preview, not general production autonomy  
**Last reviewed:** 15 October 2026

## The problem

A capable model is not automatically a safe business operator. Real work requires context, restricted permissions, tools, approval, recoverable execution and a way to distinguish attempted actions from verified outcomes.

**NEX thesis:** one adaptable AI relationship for different businesses, with capabilities driven by authorised tenant context and explicit contracts rather than isolated industry-specific clones.

## The designed experience

~~~text
Describe real work
   → reviewable operational brief
   → proposed configuration
   → bounded test / simulation
   → explicit human authority
   → attempted action and verification
   → inspectable evidence / receipt
~~~

The workflow-first interface avoids forcing users to design prompts, graph nodes or multi-agent topology before expressing their actual business problem.

## What makes the architecture different

- **Tool discovery is not authority:** a tool being available does not imply tenant access, permission or approval.
- **Model and business authority are distinct:** generated plans and interpretations cannot silently change execution policy.
- **Evidence, memory, authority and action are separate:** remembering a fact does not grant ongoing permission.
- **Contract-driven adaptation:** the same core is exercised against different synthetic tenant rules in controlled labs.
- **Verification:** adversarial and negative-control evaluation, provenance binding and explicit failure states are treated as first-class engineering concerns.

MCP is one bounded tool/adaptor seam in NEX, not the entire intelligence product.

## Evidence and maturity boundaries

Accepted internal contracts and lab paths cover adaptive behaviour, tool-policy boundaries, recovery/approval concepts and reviewable product-state semantics.

The WM3 workflow-first interface has a deterministic preview. Additional multi-turn sales, consent and policy evaluation work is staged in **Draft** branches. Neither a passing lab test nor an unmerged PR proves generally available real-world action, cross-tenant production security or customer traction.

No ISO certification or unrestricted autonomous execution is claimed.

## Roadmap

**Now:** improve comprehension of the workflow-first experience, demonstrate bounded evaluation and preserve clear permission limits.  
**Next:** controlled provider-backed execution, independent provenance checks and measurable first-value/outcome evidence.  
**Later:** approved channel/tool expansion and reusable tenant capability patterns derived from observed demand.

**My contribution:** AI product architecture, orchestration/review methodology, product and engineering alignment, evaluation design, risk/approval boundaries and evidence-oriented delivery.

This is a sanitised architectural case study, not an open-source release of the proprietary runtime.
